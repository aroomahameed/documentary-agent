📝 Stone and Splendor: The Making of Mughal Architecture

[1] Imagine a palace so beautiful, people still stop and stare at it hundreds of years later. That is the magic of Mughal architecture. Let's go on a stone-by-stone adventure to find out how it began.
🎥 Aerial shot of the Taj Mahal at sunrise, white marble glowing over the misty Yamuna river

[2] Long ago, a prince named Babur came from Central Asia. He was related to Timur and Genghis Khan. Babur took over India in 1526, and a new empire was born. His son Humayun loved Persian poetry and painting. When he came home, he brought poets and soldiers with him. Their ideas became part of Mughal design.
🎥 Painted miniature of a Mughal emperor on horseback, with a Persian poetry manuscript and calligraphy beside it

[3] Humayun's Tomb was the first big garden-tomb in India. Its garden is split into four parts, like a tiny paradise. It has pools, channels, and a huge domed building. Its style inspired builders for over a hundred years.
🎥 Humayun's Tomb in Delhi with its red sandstone and white marble dome, framed by a symmetrical charbagh garden with water channels

[4] Then came Akbar, who built a whole city of red stone near Agra. It was called Fatehpur Sikri. Its palaces, courtyards, and a great mosque were made from local red sandstone. The city was later left behind, partly because it ran short of water.
🎥 Red sandstone palaces and courtyards of Fatehpur Sikri with the Buland Darwaza gateway in the distance

[5] Then came Shah Jahan, who loved white marble. He built the Taj Mahal for his wife, Mumtaz Mahal. It took about seventeen years, from 1631 to 1648. Today, it is one of the most famous buildings on Earth. Stone can hold a love story, and it can last forever.
🎥 Close-up of the Taj Mahal's white marble dome and minarets, with a reflection in the long pool at dusk
